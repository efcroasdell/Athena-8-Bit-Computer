# Athena Clock Module — 556 Pin Verification Journal

Status: **in progress**

This journal records systematic pin-by-pin verification of the RS 305-838 dual 556 used in the Athena clock module.

The aim is not merely to confirm logic levels. Each measurement is related to the relevant internal timer mechanism so that the observed waveform can be used as evidence for the working model of the IC.

### Pin 1 — DISCHARGE1

**What it does:** Pin 1 is connected to the collector of Timer 1's internal discharge transistor. The transistor is controlled by the state of the timer's internal SR latch. When the discharge transistor is ON, it provides a low-resistance path towards ground for the timing network; when it is OFF, the timing capacitor is allowed to charge through the external resistors.

**What is connected to it:** The astable timing network. A fixed resistor connects from +5 V to pin 1, and the variable timing resistance connects from pin 1 to the joined pins 2/6 timing node.

**Expected:** A switching waveform between near ground and a high level at the astable repetition rate.

**Why:** During the charging interval, the discharge transistor is OFF and pin 1 rises through the external resistor network. When the timing capacitor reaches the upper comparator threshold, the internal latch changes state and turns the discharge transistor ON. Pin 1 is then pulled close to ground while the capacitor discharges. When the lower trigger threshold is reached, the latch changes state again and the transistor switches OFF.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **−80 mV**
- Vmax ≈ **5.12 V**
- Vpp ≈ **5.20 V**
- square switching waveform at the astable rate

The observed switching confirms that the Timer 1 discharge transistor is alternately conducting and releasing the external timing network as expected.

**Pin 1 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 2 — THRESHOLD1

**What it does:** Pin 2 is Timer 1's threshold input. Internally it feeds the upper comparator, which compares the external timing-node voltage with the upper reference generated by the timer's internal resistor divider, approximately two-thirds of VCC.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 2 is joined directly to pin 6 and to the Timer 1 timing capacitor.

**Expected:** A repeating charge/discharge waveform moving between approximately one-third and two-thirds of VCC.

**Why:** The timing capacitor charges until the threshold comparator detects that the node has reached approximately two-thirds of VCC. That comparator changes the internal latch state, which turns on the discharge transistor. The capacitor then discharges until the lower trigger comparator detects approximately one-third of VCC and reverses the latch state again.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **1.750 V**
- Vmax ≈ **3.370 V**
- Vpp ≈ **1.620 V**
- repeating ramp waveform

The measured limits closely match the expected one-third and two-thirds VCC switching region for a nominal 5 V supply.

**Pin 2 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 3 — CONTROL1

**What it does:** Pin 3 exposes Timer 1's internal control-voltage node. This node is associated with the upper comparator reference generated by the internal three-resistor divider and therefore normally sits at approximately two-thirds of VCC.

**What is connected to it:** No external control source is used in the present Athena clock configuration.

**Expected:** An essentially steady DC level near two-thirds of VCC, approximately 3.3 V on a 5 V supply.

**Why:** With no external modulation applied, the internal resistor divider establishes the comparator reference. Unlike the timing node, this voltage is not intended to ramp during normal astable operation.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **3.290 V**
- Vmax ≈ **3.370 V**
- Vpp ≈ **80 mV**
- essentially flat trace

The measured level is consistent with the expected internal two-thirds-VCC control reference.

**Pin 3 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 4 — RESET1

**What it does:** Pin 4 is the active-LOW reset input for Timer 1. Pulling this pin LOW overrides normal timer operation and forces the internal latch into the reset condition.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 4 is tied to +5 V so that Timer 1 remains enabled continuously.

**Expected:** A steady HIGH level.

**Why:** Because RESET is active LOW, holding this input HIGH permits the trigger comparator, threshold comparator, latch, discharge transistor and output stage to operate normally.

**Observed:** The trace remained firmly HIGH. Scope automatic measurements were approximately Vmin **5.40 V**, Vmax **6.00 V**, Vpp **600 mV**.

Functionally the reset input is held continuously inactive, as required for the astable oscillator.

**Pin 4 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 5 — OUTPUT1

**What it does:** Pin 5 is the output of Timer 1. Internally the output driver follows the state established by the timer's SR latch, producing the digital clock waveform generated by the astable timing cycle.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 5 is the free-running astable clock output and feeds the astable input of the SN74LS157 clock selector. An indicator LED is also associated with the astable output in the breadboard clock.

**Expected:** A square waveform with abrupt LOW and HIGH transitions.

**Why:** The timing capacitor voltage itself is analogue and ramps between the two comparator thresholds, but the comparator/latch/output chain converts those threshold crossings into a two-state digital output. Each change of latch state therefore produces a sharp transition at pin 5.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **−80 mV**
- Vmax ≈ **3.640 V**
- Vpp ≈ **3.720 V**
- square-wave output

The measured HIGH level is below VCC, which is normal for the bipolar timer output stage under load, and remains a valid TTL HIGH level.

**Pin 5 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 6 — TRIGGER1

**What it does:** Pin 6 is Timer 1's trigger input. Internally it feeds the lower comparator, which compares the timing-node voltage with the lower reference generated by the internal divider, approximately one-third of VCC.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 6 is joined directly to pin 2 and to the Timer 1 timing capacitor.

**Expected:** The same charge/discharge waveform seen on pin 2, because both pins are connected to the same physical timing node.

**Why:** During discharge, when the capacitor falls below approximately one-third of VCC, the trigger comparator changes state and sets the internal latch. That turns the discharge transistor OFF and begins the next charging interval.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **1.760 V**
- Vmax ≈ **3.320 V**
- Vpp ≈ **1.560 V**
- repeating ramp waveform matching the pin-2 timing-node behaviour

The observed lower and upper levels are consistent with the same timing capacitor node measured at pin 2.

**Pin 6 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 7 — GND

**What it does:** Pin 7 is the common ground reference for both timers inside the dual 556.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 7 is connected to the breadboard ground rail.

**Expected:** A steady level at approximately 0 V.

**Why:** The internal comparators, resistor-divider references, latches, discharge transistors and output stages all operate relative to this ground reference.

**Observed:** Approximately **160 mV** with no visible switching component in the recorded capture.

**Pin 7 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 8 — TRIGGER2

**What it does:** Pin 8 is the trigger input of Timer 2. Internally it feeds the lower trigger comparator, which compares the external trigger voltage with the lower internal reference, approximately one-third of VCC.

**What is connected to it:** In the current circuit, pin 8 is driven by **CD40106B pin 4**. The mechanical STEP switch acts on an RC node at CD40106B pin 1 (47 kΩ to +5 V, 100 nF to GND, and 1 kΩ in series with the switch to GND). Two Schmitt inverter stages condition the transition before it reaches pin 8. The earlier direct 10 kΩ pull-up and switch connection is retained below as historical verification evidence, but is no longer the current wiring.

#### STEP button released

**Expected:** Pin 8 should remain HIGH, close to +5 V.

**Why:** With pin 8 above the internal one-third-VCC trigger threshold, the trigger comparator does not set the internal SR latch. Timer 2 therefore remains in its stable state: pin 9 is LOW, the discharge transistor is active, and the timing network is ready for the next trigger. The current CD40106B output actively defines the trigger input. In the earlier direct-switch configuration, the 10 kΩ pull-up prevented the trigger input from floating.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **4.72 V**
- Vmax ≈ **5.04 V**
- Vpp ≈ **320 mV**
- essentially flat trace at about +5 V

The trigger input is safely above the internal lower comparator threshold.

**Pin 8 is behaving correctly in the idle state.**

#### STEP button pressed

**Expected:** Pin 8 should fall sharply from about +5 V to approximately 0 V and return HIGH when the button is released.

**Why:** Pressing STEP connects pin 8 directly to ground. Once the voltage falls below approximately one-third of VCC, the trigger comparator sets the internal SR latch. Setting the latch drives pin 9 HIGH and turns OFF Timer 2's discharge transistor, allowing the timing capacitor to begin charging through the external timing resistor. This is the event that starts the monostable interval.

**Observed:**

- HIGH level ≈ **5.2 V**
- LOW level ≈ **0 V**
- Vpp ≈ **5.44 V**
- clean HIGH → LOW → HIGH trigger excursion

The waveform crosses the internal trigger threshold decisively and produces the intended active-LOW trigger.

**Pin 8 is behaving correctly when triggered.**

---

### Pin 9 — OUTPUT2

**What it does:** Pin 9 is the output of Timer 2. Internally it is driven from the state of the Timer 2 SR latch through the output driver. In Athena this is the manual single-step clock pulse.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 9 feeds the manual-clock input of the SN74LS157 selector.

#### Idle state

**Expected:** Pin 9 should remain LOW while Timer 2 waits for a trigger.

**Why:** Before a trigger, the internal SR latch is in its stable reset state. The output driver therefore holds pin 9 LOW, while the discharge transistor keeps the monostable timing capacitor discharged and ready for the next event.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **−240 mV**
- Vmax ≈ **+160 mV**
- Vpp ≈ **400 mV**
- trace essentially flat around 0 V

The output is firmly within the LOW state.

**Pin 9 is behaving correctly in the idle state.**

#### Triggered state

**Expected:** Pulling pin 8 below approximately one-third of VCC should cause pin 9 to switch HIGH, remain HIGH for the monostable timing interval, and then return LOW automatically.

**Why:** The pin-8 trigger comparator sets the Timer 2 SR latch. That causes two linked internal changes: the output driver takes pin 9 HIGH, and the discharge transistor turns OFF so the external timing capacitor can charge. The timer remains in this state even after the STEP button is released.

As the timing capacitor charges, the threshold comparator monitors the timing node. When that voltage reaches approximately two-thirds of VCC, the threshold comparator resets the SR latch. The output driver then returns pin 9 LOW and the discharge transistor turns ON again, discharging the timing capacitor and restoring the stable state.

**Observed:**

- LOW level ≈ **0 V**
- HIGH level ≈ **4.32 V**
- Vpp ≈ **4.64 V**
- distinct HIGH pulses produced for successive STEP presses
- each pulse returns automatically to LOW
- a small transient overshoot is visible at the switching edges

The brief overshoot has not yet been isolated between the 556 output transition itself and the oscilloscope probing arrangement. It does not alter the valid steady TTL logic levels.

The pulse duration has not yet been recorded as a precise verified measurement in this journal.

The behaviour confirms that the trigger comparator, internal latch, discharge control, threshold path and output stage are operating together as a monostable.

**Pin 9 is behaving correctly when triggered.**

---

### Pin 10 — RESET2

**What it does:** Pin 10 is the active-LOW reset input for Timer 2. Internally RESET provides a direct override of the timer's SR latch. When driven LOW it forces the latch into the reset state regardless of the trigger and threshold comparators, driving the output LOW and preventing the monostable timing sequence. When held HIGH, that override is removed.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 10 is tied directly to +5 V so that Timer 2 remains enabled during normal operation.

**Expected:** A steady HIGH level close to VCC.

**Why:** Holding RESET HIGH allows Timer 2 to respond normally to the LOW-going trigger on pin 8 and to terminate the monostable interval when the threshold comparator detects the timing node reaching the upper reference.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **4.72 V**
- Vmax ≈ **5.04 V**
- Vpp ≈ **320 mV**
- trace essentially flat at about +5 V

The reset override is inactive and Timer 2 is enabled to operate normally.

**Pin 10 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 11 — CONTROL2

**What it does:** Pin 11 is the control-voltage input for Timer 2. Internally it exposes the upper control/reference node associated with the timer's resistor-divider network and the threshold comparator reference, nominally near two-thirds of VCC.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 11 is externally unconnected in the current circuit. It is not electrically floating internally because the 556's internal divider biases the node. A small bypass capacitor may optionally be fitted to ground for noise reduction.

**Expected:** A nearly steady voltage around two-thirds of VCC, approximately 3.3 V for a nominal 5 V supply.

**Why:** With no external control voltage applied, the internal divider establishes the control/reference level. Unlike the timing node, this point should not show the monostable charging ramp.

**Observed:**

- approximately **3.0–3.3 V**
- essentially flat trace

The measured level is consistent with the internally generated upper control/reference voltage.

**Pin 11 is behaving correctly.**

---

### Pin 12 — THRESHOLD2

**What it does:** Pin 12 is the threshold input for Timer 2. Internally it feeds the threshold comparator, which compares the timing-node voltage against the upper reference generated by the 556's internal divider, approximately two-thirds of VCC.

With a 5 V supply, that reference is approximately:

`(2/3) × 5 V ≈ 3.3 V`

When the voltage on pin 12 reaches this level, the threshold comparator resets the internal SR latch. That ends the monostable pulse, drives pin 9 LOW and turns the discharge transistor back ON.

**What is connected to it:** Pin 12 is tied externally to pin 13 at the monostable timing node. That node is connected to **1 MΩ** to +5 V and a **1 µF** timing capacitor to GND.

#### Idle state

**Expected:** Pin 12 should sit close to 0 V.

**Why:** In the stable state, the internal discharge transistor is conducting through pin 13. Because pins 12 and 13 are tied together externally, that transistor holds the timing capacitor discharged and keeps the threshold input well below the upper trip level.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **−160 mV**
- Vmax ≈ **+80 mV**
- Vpp ≈ **240 mV**
- trace essentially flat around 0 V

The timing node is being held discharged as expected.

**Pin 12 is behaving correctly at rest.**

#### Triggered state

**Expected:** When pin 8 is pulled LOW, the trigger comparator sets the internal SR latch. The discharge transistor then turns OFF, allowing the 1 µF timing capacitor to charge through the 1 MΩ resistor. Pin 12 should therefore show a rising exponential voltage from near 0 V towards approximately two-thirds of VCC. When the threshold is reached, the threshold comparator should reset the latch, causing the discharge transistor to turn ON and rapidly discharge the timing capacitor back towards ground.

**Observed:**

- Vmin ≈ **−160 mV**
- Vmax ≈ **3.20 V**
- Vpp ≈ **3.36 V**
- clear rising charging curve
- abrupt return to approximately 0 V

The measured peak of about 3.2 V is consistent with the expected upper threshold region for a nominal 5 V supply.

The waveform directly shows the monostable timing sequence: the timing capacitor charges while the discharge transistor is OFF, the threshold comparator detects the upper reference, the latch resets, and the discharge transistor rapidly returns the timing node to ground.

**Pin 12 is behaving correctly when triggered.**

## 19 September 2026 — STEP debounce verification

### Purpose

Determine why manual STEP operation sometimes produced more than one CPU clock event, implement a hardware correction based on the measured failure mechanism, and verify the correction at both the 556 trigger input and the final CPU clock.

### Pre-correction observation

DSLogic captures showed multiple short clock transitions associated with individual intended STEP actions. Oscilloscope measurements traced the disturbance backwards to 556 pin 8, where several rapid HIGH/LOW transitions were visible around a switch press.

Replacing the original switch with a 6 mm tactile switch reduced the problem substantially but did not eliminate it; a retained capture still showed an additional short CPU-clock pulse.

### Implemented conditioning

A CD40106BE Schmitt-trigger inverter was added with:

- pin 1 → 47 kΩ → +5 V;
- pin 1 → 100 nF → GND;
- pin 1 → 1 kΩ → STEP switch → GND;
- pin 2 → pin 3;
- pin 4 → 556 pin 8.

Two inverter stages preserve the original active-LOW trigger polarity.

### Oscilloscope result

Dual-channel observation used:

- yellow: CD40106B pin 1, the RC node;
- blue: 556 pin 8, the conditioned trigger.

Observed:

- the RC node transitions between defined HIGH and LOW levels;
- the 556 trigger changes cleanly from HIGH to LOW;
- no visible contact chatter remains at pin 8 at the test timebase.

This verifies the local electrical function of the debounce stage.

### Logic-analyser result

A retained post-debounce DSLogic capture showed deliberate manual STEP actions producing one clean CPU clock pulse each, with no additional short chatter pulses.

**Observed fact:** the implemented RC + CD40106B stage removed the previously visible repeated CPU-clock events in the captured test.

**Acceptance result:** manual STEP input debouncing and clean single-clock generation are verified for the tested configuration.

## 19 September 2026 — RUN/MANUAL propagation recheck

An apparent transient observation suggested that a manual STEP might be propagating while RUN was selected. The clock path was therefore checked stage by stage.

Measured:

- SN74LS157 pin 1 SELECT: **0 V in MANUAL**, approximately **3.6 V in RUN**;
- SN74LS157 pin 4 selected-clock output: no STEP-correlated change in RUN;
- SN7400 pin 9: no STEP-correlated change in RUN;
- SN7400 pin 11 final CPU clock: no STEP-correlated change in RUN.

**Conclusion:** the current selector and final clock-gating path reject the manual STEP source correctly in RUN mode. The earlier observation could not be reproduced after the temporary test wiring had been stabilised.
